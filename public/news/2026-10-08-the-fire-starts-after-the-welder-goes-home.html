Most Hot Work Fires Start After the Welder Has Gone Home

Fire Protection October 8, 2026

Ask anyone who has investigated a hot work fire when it started and the answer is almost never "during the welding". It started during the welding. It was discovered four hours later, after the crew had packed up, signed off and left the building.

Why the delay happens

A grinding spark or a globule of weld spatter carries enough energy to ignite dust, lagging, cardboard or oil-soaked rag, but not enough to produce a flame straight away. What it produces is a smoulder: a slow, oxygen-starved burn inside insulation or behind a panel, invisible and almost odourless at first. It can sit like that for hours. Then it reaches better air, and the smoulder becomes a fire in a building nobody is standing in.

Sparks also travel much further than people assume. They roll. They bounce. They fall through floor grating, gaps around pipe penetrations and cable trays into the room below — a room the permit never mentioned, because the person writing it was looking at the work, not underneath it.

What an effective permit controls

  • A cleared radius, not a cleared bench. Combustibles removed or covered for a proper distance in every direction, including below and behind.
  • Openings sealed. Floor penetrations, drains and cable routes closed before the first spark, not after.
  • The space below checked and occupied. Somebody physically looks at the room underneath.
  • Extinguishing media at the work, in hand. Not the nearest extinguisher down the corridor.
  • A fire watch during the work and for a defined period after it — commonly at least an hour, longer where insulation or dust is present. The watch does nothing else, and cannot be the person who did the welding.
  • A closing inspection that someone signs, after the watch period, before the permit is cancelled.

Where permits fail in practice

They fail at the end, not the beginning. The precautions get taken, the job goes fine, and then the watch gets cut short because the shift is over or the welder is needed elsewhere. The permit is signed off on the assumption that an uneventful job is a finished one.

It is not. The hour after the work is the hour the fire chooses.

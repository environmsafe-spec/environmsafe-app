A Respirator Nobody Fit Tested Is Decoration

Training & Competence October 31, 2026

Respiratory protection is the only PPE whose failure is invisible to the person relying on it. A hard hat that is too small is obvious. A respirator that leaks feels exactly like a respirator that does not, and the wearer walks into the atmosphere confident and unprotected.

Fit is individual, not a size on a box

Tight-fitting masks seal against the face, and faces differ. The same model that seals perfectly on one technician leaks on the colleague standing next to them. That is why fit testing exists: it is a measurement on a named person wearing a specific make, model and size, and the result transfers to nobody else.

A fit test record names the person, the equipment and the date. A box of masks with a CE mark on it records nothing about whether anyone on your site is actually protected.

What destroys a seal

  • Facial hair at the sealing surface. Not a style question — stubble of a day or two measurably breaks the seal, and no strap tension compensates.
  • Spectacle arms passing through the seal on full-face masks.
  • Weight change or dental work altering face shape since the last test.
  • Perished straps and face seals on masks kept in a drawer between uses.
  • Wrong filter for the hazard, correctly fitted to a perfectly sealing mask.

The checks that belong to the wearer

A fit test is periodic. A pre-use seal check is every single time, takes seconds, and is the thing most often skipped: cover the filter, inhale, and feel whether the mask pulls onto the face and holds. If it does not, the mask is not working today regardless of what the certificate says.

Where it belongs in the hierarchy

RPE is the last line, not the first. If a task needs a respirator, the first question is whether the exposure can be removed, substituted, enclosed or ventilated away. Reaching for a mask is sometimes correct and often a sign that an earlier control was never considered.

A Bearing Announces Itself Months Before It Fails

Inspection & Monitoring November 13, 2026

When a pump or a fan fails, the account afterwards is usually that it went without warning. It almost never did. Rotating equipment degrades along a fairly predictable path, and the earliest stages are measurable long before anything is audible or hot.

The order things become detectable

A developing bearing fault shows up first as a change in high-frequency vibration, weeks or months ahead. Later it becomes audible. Later still it runs hot. By the time a technician on a walk-round can hear or feel it, most of the useful warning has already been spent.

That order is why a walk-round, however diligent, is a late detector. It is not a criticism of the person walking round; the information simply is not available to a hand and an ear yet.

What the warning buys you

Not avoided maintenance - the bearing still has to be changed. What it buys is choosing when. A planned change is a scheduled shutdown, the right part in stock, a day shift and an hour of work. The same bearing failing on its own terms is an unplanned outage, a damaged shaft, a courier charge and a night crew.

What to measure, and how often

  • Overall vibration on the critical machines, at the same points each time, trended rather than compared to a single limit.
  • Temperature at bearing housings, which is a second, later signal that confirms the first.
  • A baseline taken when the machine is known good. Without it a reading is a number with nothing to compare against, which is the usual reason monitoring programmes produce nothing.
  • Enough frequency that a trend exists. Two readings a year is not a trend.

Start with the machines whose failure stops production or safety systems, not with everything that rotates. A short list measured properly beats a long list measured occasionally.

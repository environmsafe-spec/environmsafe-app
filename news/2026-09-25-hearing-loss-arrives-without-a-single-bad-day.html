Hearing Loss Arrives Without a Single Bad Day

Occupational Health September 25, 2026

Almost every other injury on a site announces itself. Noise does not. Hearing loss from workplace noise builds over years, painlessly, and by the time someone notices they are asking people to repeat themselves, the damage is permanent. There is no treatment that restores it.

How loud is too loud

Damage depends on both level and time. As sound level rises, the time it takes to do harm falls sharply, which is why a short burst from a grinder or a hammer on steel can matter as much as a full shift beside a generator.

Two rough field checks are worth knowing. If people have to raise their voices to be understood about a metre apart, the area is likely to need assessment. If workers have ringing in the ears or muffled hearing at the end of a shift, exposure is already too high, even though hearing usually recovers by morning in the early years.

Protect at the source first

Hearing protection is the last line, not the first. In order of effectiveness:

  • Quieter equipment. Noise output is a purchasing specification. It is far cheaper to buy a quieter compressor than to manage the noise it makes for the next fifteen years.
  • Maintenance. Worn bearings, loose guards, unbalanced fans and failing silencers add noise that was never in the design.
  • Enclosure and separation. Acoustic housings, screens, and simply moving people or machines so fewer workers are inside the noisy zone.
  • Time. Rotating tasks and limiting how long anyone spends in the loudest areas.

Why protectors under-perform

Earplugs and muffs are rated in laboratories on well-fitted samples. In practice the protection achieved is usually far lower. Foam plugs that are not rolled and inserted properly sit in the outer ear and do very little. Muffs leak where spectacle arms, long hair or a respirator strap break the seal. Protection worn for most of a shift but removed for ten minutes in the loudest area loses most of its value, because the exposure that remains dominates the day.

Fit, training and the right type for the job matter more than the number printed on the box.

Audiometry is monitoring, not protection

Regular hearing tests do not prevent damage. What they do is detect the earliest shift in someone's hearing while the loss is still small, so the exposure behind it can be found and fixed before it becomes disabling. A baseline test on starting work, and tests at regular intervals afterwards, turn hearing conservation into something measurable rather than assumed.

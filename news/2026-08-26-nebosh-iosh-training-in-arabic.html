NEBOSH and IOSH Training in Arabic: Competence Without the Language Barrier

Training Services August 26, 2026

Safety training fails quietly. A workforce sits through a course, signs the register, and goes back to exactly the same habits. It is rarely because people are careless. More often the material never landed, and across Yemen and the wider region one of the most common reasons is simple: the training was delivered in a language the workforce does not think in.

The certificate is not the point

A certificate proves attendance. It does not prove that a supervisor can spot an unsafe lift, challenge a permit that does not match the job, or explain to a new hire why an isolation matters. That gap between paperwork and competence is where incidents live, and it widens every time content is delivered in a second language to people who will apply it in their first.

Accredited programmes we deliver

EnvironmSafe is a NEBOSH and IOSH accredited training provider. Our programmes include:

  • NEBOSH International General Certificate — available through both Arabic and English pathways, the standard qualification for anyone with day-to-day safety responsibility.
  • NEBOSH Process Safety Management — aimed at oil, gas and process operations where the hazards are systemic rather than individual.
  • NEBOSH Level 6 International Diploma — the route toward chartered practice for career safety professionals.
  • NEBOSH HSE Introduction to Incident Investigation — how to find the cause rather than the culprit.
  • IOSH Working Safely — a single day, appropriate for the entire workforce regardless of role.
  • IOSH Managing Safely — for supervisors and managers who carry responsibility for others.

Why the Arabic pathway matters

An internationally recognised qualification loses none of its standing when it is taught and examined in Arabic. What changes is retention. Candidates ask better questions, argue with the material, and connect it to their own site rather than memorising it for an exam. Supervisors who trained in Arabic are measurably more willing to intervene, because intervening requires confidence in the words as much as in the rule.

For organisations working with international clients and NGOs, this also removes a false trade-off. You do not have to choose between a credential your partners recognise and training your workforce genuinely absorbs.

In practice most sites end up with mixed teams, and that is fine. Running the Arabic pathway for operators and supervisors while senior or client-facing staff take the English route keeps one common standard across the organisation, without asking anyone to learn safety in a second language under exam pressure.

Training that survives contact with the site

The test of a programme is what happens six months later, when nobody is watching and the shortcut is available. We build training around the hazards our clients actually face — lifting operations, confined spaces, hot work, LPG and fire risk, contractor control — so the examples on the screen match the equipment outside the door.

If your last training round produced certificates but no visible change in behaviour, the problem is usually the design, not the people.

Training your team will actually use

We deliver NEBOSH and IOSH accredited programmes in Arabic and English, built around the hazards on your site rather than a generic syllabus.
